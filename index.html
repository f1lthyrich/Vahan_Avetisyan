The Artist

A Voice Carved
from Apricot Wood

Vahan Avetisyan is a Los Angeles-based Armenian musician — a living bridge between the ancient highlands of Armenia and the stages of the United States. A master of six wind instruments, his soul is rooted in centuries-old traditions of his homeland.

Whether breathing life into the duduk — Armenia's most sacred instrument, UNESCO-recognized — or igniting a room with the saxophone, Vahan commands every stage with the authority of a craftsman who has devoted his life to the art.

Based in Glendale, California, Vahan performs at weddings, cultural events, concerts, and private occasions — anywhere music has the power to move souls across generations.

The Instruments

Six Voices,
One Master

01🪈ԴուդուկDuduk

Armenia's most sacred instrument. A double-reed woodwind carved from apricot wood, UNESCO Intangible Heritage of Humanity. The soul of an entire civilization in a single breath.

02🎷ՍաքսոֆոնSaxophone

From jazz-inflected pop to Armenian folk fusion. The saxophone in Vahan's hands becomes entirely his own — soulful, commanding, impossible to ignore at any event.

03🎵ՓկուPku

An ancient single-reed horn of Armenian origin with a piercing, nasal tone unlike any other instrument on earth. Rare. Elemental. In very few hands worldwide.

04📯ԶուռնաZourna

The outdoor conical oboe of the Caucasus. Loud, jubilant, built for open-air weddings and celebrations. When the zourna plays, nobody stays seated.

05🎶ՇվիShvi

A delicate end-blown Armenian folk flute producing a soft, breathy, ethereal sound. The quiet whisper of the Armenian mountains — meditative and deeply spiritual.

🎺ԿլառնետClarinet

Bridging classical and folk traditions, Vahan's clarinet spans Armenian folk songs, classical repertoire, and contemporary fusion — a testament to his formal training and boundless musical range. Together with his five other instruments, the clarinet completes a toolkit for any style, any occasion, any stage in the world.
